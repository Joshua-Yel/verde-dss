# VERDE User Manual

## Welcome to VERDE

VERDE is your business operations dashboard. It helps you understand how your salon or service business is performing, what is selling well, what inventory needs attention, how much staffing you need, and what is likely to happen next month.

This is not just a reporting screen. It is a working decision tool for:

- revenue tracking
- service demand planning
- inventory monitoring
- staffing coverage
- expense and margin tracking
- AI-assisted business questions

You can use it from a desktop browser and work from a single dashboard that brings together the daily business picture.

---

## 1. What the system is for

VERDE helps you answer questions like:

- Are sales growing or falling?
- Which services bring in the most revenue?
- Which services are getting busier?
- Are we running low on products?
- Do we need to reorder inventory?
- Are we staffing enough for busy times?
- How much money will we make next month?
- What should we focus on this week?

The dashboard turns raw business activity into simple, useful decisions.

---

## 2. Who can use it

The system is organized by role so people only see the areas relevant to them.

### Common user roles

- Owner or manager: sees the full system and can manage settings
- Finance team: focuses on revenue, expenses, margins, and forecasts
- Staff: sees staffing and labor planning information
- Inventory team: sees stock and reorder information
- General user: sees overview and core operational information

Not every person sees every page. The system hides tools that are not needed for their role.

## 5. Overview page: the big picture

The Overview page is the first dashboard you usually see. It is the executive summary of your business.

This page helps answer:

- Are things stable or changing?
- Which services matter most?
- Is the business generating healthy revenue?
- Are there inventory or staffing risks?

### What you will see

#### KPI cards

These are the summary scorecards across the top of the page.

Typical KPI cards include:

- Total Revenue
- Average Daily Revenue
- Total Net Income
- Sessions / bookings count
- Current forecast
- Most booked service
- Low stock item count

These cards are designed to give you instant context without reading a full report.

### Revenue Run Rate chart

This is the main trend chart.

What it shows:

- actual revenue over time
- forecast line showing expected trend
- whether performance is tracking above or below the usual pattern

How to read it:

- rising line = revenue is growing
- flat line = stable performance
- falling line = revenue is shrinking
- gray forecast line = expected trend based on recent behavior

### Revenue allocation chart

This is a service mix chart.

What it shows:

- how much each service contributes to total revenue
- which services are the largest contributors
- which service lines are carrying the business

This is useful when deciding:

- where to push marketing
- which service should be highlighted
- where training or staffing needs improvement

### Daily log stream

This is a list of recent day-by-day business activity.

It usually includes:

- date
- booked appointments or sessions
- revenue
- high-level service mix
- other business activity summaries

It is useful for checking what happened day by day instead of only looking at monthly totals.

---

## 6. Service Demand page

The Service Demand page shows customer demand by service.

This is the place to learn:

- which services are getting more demand
- which services are slowing down
- which ones should be promoted or adjusted

### What the page is for

Use this page when you want to answer:

- Are more people booking haircuts, color, styling, or other services?
- Which service lines are growing fastest?
- How much demand is expected next month?

### What you will see

- A demand chart over time
- Service forecast lines
- A table of services ranked by demand or contribution
- Forecast model options

### Reading the service chart

- upward trend = growing demand
- downward trend = declining demand
- spiky periods = seasonal or campaign-driven demand

This helps you align staffing, inventory, and marketing with the actual service demand.

### Forecast model options

The system may let you switch between different forecasting methods, such as:

- weighted moving average
- simple moving average
- naive forecast

The goal is to compare what the recent trend suggests compared with recent history.

---

## 7. Inventory page

The Inventory page is about stock health.

This page helps you answer:

- Which items are running low?
- Which items need reordering?
- What is likely to be left in stock next month?
- Are we overstocked or understocked?

### What you will see

- stock amounts
- reorder points
- average usage
- current status
- projected next month stock
- reorder suggestions

### Low stock alerts

Items close to or below the reorder point are highlighted. This is meant to prevent running out of core products or supplies.

### Reorder recommendations

The system may suggest how much to reorder based on current stock and expected usage. If you see an item marked as urgent, it usually means:

- stock is low
- demand is ongoing
- without action, the item may run out soon

### Inventory forecasting

This page looks ahead to what stock levels may look like next month. That helps with purchasing decisions and helps prevent both:

- stockouts
- excess inventory

---

## 8. Financials page

This section is for the business numbers behind the operation.

It helps answer:

- Are we making money?
- Is revenue increasing faster than expenses?
- What will our net income look like next month?
- What is the forecast for the next few periods?

### What you will see

- revenue trend
- expense trend
- net income view
- projected financials

### How to interpret it

- Revenue line rising = sales are growing
- Expense line rising too quickly = costs are increasing faster than sales
- Net income line dropping = profit is being squeezed
- Large gap between revenue and expenses = potential margin risk

### Best use

Check this page when you need to plan budgets, pricing, or cost management.

---

## 9. Staffing page

The Staffing page is used to match labor with service demand.

This helps answer:

- Are we understaffed during busy periods?
- Do we have too much labor during quiet periods?
- Which days or times are busiest?

### What it shows

- daily or weekly service demand patterns
- service trends across days
- staffing intensity by weekday or time period
- areas where staffing may be too low or too high

### Why it matters

If demand is high but staffing is low, service may slow down, customers may wait longer, and revenue may be lost. If staffing is high during quiet periods, labor costs can increase without value.

This page is used to make smarter schedule decisions.

## 11. Importing data

The system depends on accurate historical data. You can import business data from Excel files.

### When to import

Use it when:

- new data has arrived
- a month or week was not yet recorded
- you changed spreadsheet formats
- you are setting up the system from an external file

### How it works

1. Click the Import Data button.
2. Choose the spreadsheet file.
3. Select the type of data being imported:
   - operations data
   - inventory data
   - expense data
4. Match columns to the correct fields.
5. Review the imported rows.
6. Submit the file.

### Why this matters

If the import is clean, the reports, forecasts, and charts are more accurate. Bad or missing data can distort dashboards and forecasts.

## 12. Reports and exporting data

The system includes a Reports feature so you can save the dashboard in a clean, shareable format.

### Where to find it

1. Open the left sidebar.
2. Look for the Reports section.
3. Click Reports.
4. Choose the sections you want to include.

### What you can export

You can choose from these sections:

- Overview
- Service Demand
- Inventory
- Financials
- Staffing

This allows you to export only the information that matters for the meeting, review, or decision you are making.

### Choose a file format

You can export as:

- PDF for sharing, printing, or presentation use
- Excel for editing, filtering, and deeper analysis in spreadsheet tools

### Best uses

Use PDF when you want:

- a clean snapshot for leadership
- a printable business review
- a summary for meetings or handoff notes

Use Excel when you want:

- to keep the raw data for analysis
- to send a file to someone who prefers spreadsheets
- to work with numbers outside the dashboard

### Typical export workflow

1. Click Reports in the sidebar.
2. Select the sections you want to include.
3. Choose PDF or Excel.
4. Click Export.
5. The file downloads to your device.

### Important note

The report is built from the sections you selected. If you pick only a few sections, the export will be focused and smaller. If you want a broad management summary, select all sections.

This is especially useful for weekly reviews, monthly business checks, leadership updates, or sharing workload and financial performance with others.

## 13. The AI assistant: ARIA

VERDE includes an AI assistant named ARIA.

This tool helps you ask business questions in plain language instead of manually digging through reports.

### Examples of questions to ask

- Why did revenue rise last month?
- Which services are trending up?
- Which items should be reordered?
- What is the biggest staffing risk this week?
- What is the forecast for next month?
- What should we focus on to improve profitability?

### How to use it

1. Click the AI button in the top bar.
2. Ask a question in normal language.
3. Read the answer.
4. Use the response with the charts and KPI cards to make a decision.

### Best practice

Ask specific but practical questions such as:

- “Which service had the strongest growth this quarter?”
- “What inventory is low?”
- “How much staff coverage do we likely need next month?”

The AI is meant to support decisions, not replace management judgment.

## 14. Reading the charts correctly

### Line charts

Use line charts for trends over time.

Ask:

- Is it trending up or down?
- Is this a temporary spike or a pattern?
- Is the latest value better or worse than previous periods?

### Pie or allocation charts

Use these to understand which services or categories dominate the business.

Ask:

- Which service makes the most money?
- Where are our biggest opportunities?
- Is one service carrying too much of the business?

### Tables

Use tables when you need exact numbers.

Ask:

- Which item is low?
- Which service is hottest?
- What is the expected next-month revenue?

### Heatmaps and staffing patterns

Use these to see where activity is most intense.

Ask:

- Which day is busiest?
- Which time block is most demanding?
- Do we need more or fewer staff at that moment?

## 15. Daily workflow example

Here is a simple example of how a manager might use the system:

### Morning check

1. Open Overview.
2. Check revenue trend and KPI cards.
3. See if the business is on track.

### Service review

1. Open Service Demand.
2. Check which services are rising.
3. Decide whether to push or staff up a service line.

### Inventory check

1. Open Inventory.
2. Look for items below reorder point.
3. Place an order before stock runs out.

### Staffing review

1. Go to Staffing.
2. Look at the busiest periods.
3. Adjust staffing schedules accordingly.

### Financial check

1. Open Financials.
2. Review revenue vs expense and projected net income.
3. Decide whether to tighten spending or push sales in a category.

### Ask the AI

1. Use ARIA to ask a quick question.
2. Cross-check the answer with the charts.
3. Make the final operation decision.

## 16. Best practices for using the system

### Check the dashboard regularly

The more often you review the dashboard, the easier it is to spot trends early.

### Keep imports current

If the data is stale, the reports and forecasts will be less useful.

### Use the system for decisions, not just reporting

The goal is not just to see numbers. The goal is to act on them.

### Watch signals early

Low-stock warnings, declining demand, and falling profit margins are early signs that should trigger action.

### Combine numbers with judgment

The dashboard tells you what is happening and what is likely to happen. You still make the final decision.
