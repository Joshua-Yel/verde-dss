# VERDE Staff Training Guide

## Purpose of this guide

This guide is designed for staff members who use VERDE as part of their daily work. It explains how to log in, what each area is for, how to interpret the dashboard, and how to use the system in a way that helps operations run smoothly.

The goal is not to make you a data expert. The goal is to help you understand:

- where to find the information you need
- what the dashboard is telling you
- how your work connects to revenue, staffing, and service demand
- how to use the tools correctly without confusion

## ARIA: your built-in assistant

VERDE includes an AI assistant named ARIA. ARIA helps staff ask natural-language questions about the business without digging through every page manually.

Use ARIA for quick questions such as:

- Which service is busiest this week?
- What inventory should we check today?
- Which day needs more staffing?
- What trend should we watch next?

ARIA is meant to support staff decisions, not replace manager judgment or team review.

---

## 1. What VERDE is

VERDE is a business operations dashboard for a salon or service-based business. It helps the business understand:

- how much revenue is coming in
- which services are growing or slowing down
- which products or supplies are low
- whether staffing is matched to demand
- how different parts of the business are performing over time

In practical terms, VERDE is the place where staff and managers look at the health of the business.

## 2. Who this training is for

This guide is for:

- front-of-house staff
- service team members
- inventory coordinators
- support staff

- anyone who needs to understand daily operations and reports

Even if you are not using every page, it is helpful to know how the dashboard fits together.

## 4. Logging in

### Step-by-step

1. Open the VERDE app in your browser.
2. Go to the login page.
3. Enter your email and password.
4. Sign in.
5. Wait for the dashboard to load.

### After login

You will see the main navigation menu on the left side of the screen. The menu shows the sections available to your role.

If you do not see a section, it may be restricted to another role, such as manager or finance.

---

## 5. The main screen layout

The dashboard has a simple structure.

### Left sidebar

This is the main navigation area. It includes the main business modules, such as:

- Overview
- Service Demand
- Inventory
- Financials
- Staffing
- Admin
- Reports

### Top bar

The top bar shows useful actions such as:

- date range information
- import data button
- AI assistant button

### Main content area

This is where charts, KPI cards, tables, and operational summaries appear.

---

## 6. Overview page: the main dashboard

The Overview page is the first place most people look.

This page is the business snapshot for the day or current reporting period.

### What it tells you

It helps answer questions like:

- Are we doing well this month?
- Are sales rising or falling?
- Which services are most important right now?
- Are we seeing operational risk in inventory or staffing?

### KPI cards

KPI cards are summary tiles that show fast numbers, such as:

- total revenue
- average daily revenue
- total net income
- number of sessions or bookings
- forecast status
- most booked service
- low stock alerts

### What staff should notice

Staff do not need to memorize every KPI, but they should understand the basics:

- higher revenue is usually positive
- falling revenue can signal a drop in demand
- low stock items can cause service disruption
- staffing shortages can create customer delays

---

## 7. Reading the charts and data correctly

### Revenue trend chart

This shows how revenue is moving over time.

What to look for:

- rising line = revenue is increasing
- flat line = stable business performance
- falling line = slow period or trouble area

This chart tells you whether the business is trending up or down.

### Service contribution chart

This is about service mix. It shows which services are making the biggest contribution to the business.

What to look for:

- services with the biggest slice are the most valuable to the business
- a small service might still be very important if it drives traffic or upsells
- if one service is shrinking, it may need attention

### Daily activity log

This shows recent daily operating information.

What to look for:

- specific days with higher revenue
- spikes in service activity
- unusual slow periods

This helps explain what changed in the business over time.

---

## 8. Service Demand page

The Service Demand page explains how busy each service is and whether demand is growing or falling.

### Why this matters to staff

This page helps the business understand real client demand. It helps answer:

- which services are getting more bookings?
- which services are falling behind?
- what should be promoted?
- which services need more staffing support?

### What to look for

- rising demand means more customer interest
- falling demand may mean need for marketing or service changes
- spike periods may mean seasonality or campaign effects

### How this impacts operations

If one service is doing very well, the business may need:

- more staff trained in that service
- more inventory for that service
- better scheduling
- more communication to customers

---

## 9. Inventory page

The Inventory page is used to track products and supply levels.

### Why this matters to staff

If the business runs out of products or supplies, service delivery can be disrupted.

This page helps answer:

- Are we low on key items?
- Which stock items need to be reordered?
- Are we holding too much stock of something?

### What staff should look for

- red or low-stock warnings
- items near or under reorder point
- items with poor projected stock levels for the next month

### Typical action

If an item is low:

1. note it
2. inform the right person
3. help trigger a reorder
4. avoid service delays

### Why this is important

A salon or service business often depends on products and consumables. A low product level can affect quality, customer experience, and sales.

---

## 10. Financials page

The Financials page looks at revenue, cost, and profit trends.

### Why staff should understand it

Even if you are not in finance, understanding whether the business is healthy helps you understand pressure points.

### What it shows

- revenue trend
- expense trend
- net income or profit trend
- projected financial performance

### How to read it

- rising revenue is positive
- rising expenses without matching revenue is a risk
- falling net income means profit is shrinking

This page helps managers decide budget and pricing actions, but staff should still know the business is monitoring margins and sustainability.

---

## 11. Staffing page

The Staffing page is used to align labor with service demand.

### Why this matters to staff

This page helps the business determine whether the schedule is balanced.

### What it shows

- busiest days
- busiest service periods
- staffing intensity by day or time
- areas where staffing may be too low or too high

### How to use it

If demand is high:

- more coverage may be needed
- support or cross-training may be helpful

If demand is low:

- scheduling can be adjusted
- labor costs can be controlled

This helps create better coverage without overstaffing.

---

## 12. Admin page

The Admin page is not the main operational dashboard. It is the internal control area for the system.

### Who uses it

This area is mainly for:

- owners
- managers
- administrators
- system administrators

### What it helps manage

- user access
- workspace setup
- role permissions
- AI configuration
- business settings

Most staff members will not need to work here regularly.

---

## 13. Reports and exporting data

The Reports feature lets you download the dashboard information for sharing or review.

### Where to find it

1. Open the left sidebar.
2. Click Reports.
3. Select the sections you want to include.
4. Choose a format.
5. Export the file.

### Formats available

- PDF: best for presentation and print-friendly sharing
- Excel: best for spreadsheets and deeper analysis

### Why staff should know this

Sometimes managers or leadership need a summary outside the system. Reporting helps you share:

- sales performance
- inventory issues
- staffing needs
- service trends

### Best practice

Use a PDF when you need a clean snapshot for a meeting. Use Excel when someone needs to change or analyze the numbers.

## 15. Daily responsibilities for staff

A staff member should use the system in a simple, practical way.

### Daily checklist

At the start of a day or week, you might:

1. open Overview
2. check the current revenue and service trends
3. review any low inventory items
4. check if a service is becoming busier
5. note staffing risk areas
6. use ARIA if you need a quick answer

### What you are looking for

- signs of an unusually slow day
- low inventory items that may affect service
- staffing stress points
- service demand changes that need attention

---

## 16. What staff should not do

Avoid these common mistakes:

- ignoring low-stock warnings
- assuming a single week tells the whole story
- treating the dashboard as a final answer instead of a decision aid
- focusing only on revenue without looking at inventory and staffing
- forgetting that the data must be current to be useful

---

## 17. How the data connects to daily work

The dashboard is not random data. It reflects real business activity.

### Example

If demand for one service rises:

- more clients may book that service
- the business may need more staffing or product support
- the service demand page will reflect the trend
- management may decide to adjust marketing or scheduling

If inventory drops:

- low-stock alerts appear
- product can be reordered before running out
- service quality is protected

If staffing is weak for a busy period:

- the schedule can be adjusted
- customer wait times can be reduced
- service delivery improves

This is why each page is connected to real operations.

## 18. Common staff questions

### “Do I need to understand every chart?”

No. Focus on the charts that relate to your work. For example:

- staff may focus on staffing and demand
- inventory staff focus on stock levels
- front-of-house may focus on service demand and revenue trends

### “Do I need to export reports?”

Sometimes, but not every day. Leadership or managers often do this for reviews.

### “What is the most important page for me?”

If you are on the service team, the most useful pages are often Overview and Service Demand. If you handle stock, Inventory is most important.

### “What if I don’t know what a chart means?”

Use ARIA or ask a manager. The main idea is to look for trends: up, down, stable, or risk.
